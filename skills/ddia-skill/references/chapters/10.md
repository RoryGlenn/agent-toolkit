# Chapter 10. Consistency and Consensus

Original applied guidance, not copied book text. These procedures are engineering deductions; retrieve the cited source sections before attributing a consequential claim to DDIA. Verify product/version guarantees independently.

## Source orientation

- Linearizability makes individual operations appear atomic on one current copy and respects real-time precedence. Serializability orders multi-object transactions but permits a different order from real time; their combination is strict serializability.
- Strong recency matters for exclusive ownership, immediate uniqueness decisions, and cross-channel dependencies such as an object write followed by a message referring to it. It may be unnecessary for cached service discovery or applications that tolerate temporary staleness.
- Neither quorum overlap alone nor the presence of a consensus algorithm proves every read is linearizable. Stale leaders, asynchronous failover, weak read paths, and timestamp-based conflict resolution can violate the guarantee.
- Consensus provides agreement despite faults under an explicit model, at the cost of coordination and limited progress when a quorum is unavailable. Proven implementations are preferable to ad hoc leader election; committed log preservation and reconfiguration are subtle.
- Lamport and hybrid logical timestamps preserve causality observed through messages, but do not guarantee real-time recency or identify all concurrent events. Vector clocks can identify concurrency with additional metadata. Unique and approximately sortable IDs are different requirements.
- CAP concerns linearizability and availability during partitions, not a universal pick-two design menu. Discuss the exact operation, failure, and latency tradeoff. Fetch-and-add has consensus number 2; CAS and shared logs support any number of proposers.

## Linearizability, serializability and real-time order

Concept: `consistency-contracts`.

**Apply when:** Specify correctness of reads, transactions, uniqueness or ownership.

**Procedure:** Write an operation history with invocation/completion intervals and the required business invariant. Separate single-object real-time ordering from serial transaction equivalence; determine whether strict serializability or a weaker guarantee is sufficient.

**Qualifications:** Serializability alone does not require real-time order. An implementation label is not evidence that all accessed paths share the guarantee.

**Incorrect example:** Use serializable analytics transactions as proof that a cache always reflects the latest completed write.

**Sufficient example:** Specify and test the actual read path guarantee independently from transaction isolation.

**Falsification check:** Construct histories with a completed write followed by another client read and concurrent multi-record transactions; check the claimed property.

**Source sections:** `OEBPS/ch10.html#sec_consistency_lin_definition`, `OEBPS/ch10.html#sidebar_consistency_serializability`.

## Partition behavior and cross-channel causality

Concept: `partition-tradeoffs`.

**Apply when:** Choose regional availability or coordinate across storage and messaging.

**Procedure:** Identify exact operations, partition types and invariant that requires coordination. Trace causal dependencies across channels such as object upload and message publication. Decide where to reject, wait, allocate rights or accept weaker semantics.

**Qualifications:** CAP is about particular consistency/availability definitions during partitions, not choosing two features forever. Pending or compensatable business states require explicit acceptance.

**Incorrect example:** Accept the same last inventory unit in both isolated regions and promise immediate global no-oversell.

**Sufficient example:** Coordinate admission or preallocate exclusive rights, exposing the chosen availability tradeoff.

**Falsification check:** Partition regions and reorder upload/notification visibility; verify the accepted business behavior.

**Source sections:** `OEBPS/ch10.html#sec_consistency_cap`, `OEBPS/ch10.html#sec_consistency_multi_channel`.

## Logical clocks and ID generator requirements

Concept: `logical-identities`.

**Apply when:** Choose identifiers or infer order from timestamps.

**Procedure:** Separate uniqueness, total order, causality, real-time precedence and gaplessness. Compare random IDs, local sequences, Lamport/HLC/vector metadata and coordinated allocators according to the actual requirement. Consider failover, rollback and clock behavior.

**Qualifications:** Lamport/HLC ordering does not detect all concurrency like vectors and does not inherently prove real-time latest state. Unique is not gapless.

**Incorrect example:** Use random unique IDs as a causal sequence for privacy policy updates and subsequent content publication.

**Sufficient example:** Carry the required causal dependency or use a suitable ordered authority; use simpler unique IDs where order is irrelevant.

**Falsification check:** Generate concurrently during failover and clock shifts; check each stated identity/order property separately.

**Source sections:** `OEBPS/ch10.html#sec_consistency_timestamps`, `OEBPS/ch10.html#sec_consistency_linearizable_id`.

## Consensus, shared logs and membership

Concept: `consensus-mechanisms`.

**Apply when:** Use a leader, replicated log or membership change for correctness.

**Procedure:** Identify what value/order is agreed and when it is committed. Inspect durable quorum, term/epoch, stale leader rejection and reconfiguration semantics. Distinguish local atomic primitives, atomic commit and replicated consensus.

**Qualifications:** Leader-based replication without safe election is not automatically consensus. Fetch-and-add has consensus number two in the discussed model; CAS/shared-log connections are stronger.

**Incorrect example:** Replace voting membership on every node independently and assume majority safety survives.

**Sufficient example:** Use the protocol-supported membership transition and retain quorum intersection as required.

**Falsification check:** Exercise leader loss and membership transition with delayed messages; verify no conflicting committed histories.

**Source sections:** `OEBPS/ch10.html#sec_consistency_faces`, `OEBPS/ch10.html#sec_consistency_consensus_leader`, `OEBPS/ch10.html#id283`.

## Coordination services and operational boundaries

Concept: `coordination-use`.

**Apply when:** Use coordination for configuration, allocation or discovery.

**Procedure:** Keep low-volume coordination separate from bulk data paths. Define ownership epochs, watch/session expiry and how downstream resources validate decisions. Test stale caches and discovery updates.

**Qualifications:** A discovery result can be stale. A coordination service does not make a separately written database operation atomic.

**Incorrect example:** Treat a watch callback as permanent permission to write, even after session expiration.

**Sufficient example:** Attach validated ownership epochs to resource operations and handle session/reconfiguration events explicitly.

**Falsification check:** Expire a session while consumers retain cached assignments; verify stale work cannot violate ownership.

**Source sections:** `OEBPS/ch10.html#sec_consistency_coordination`, `OEBPS/ch10.html#id286`, `OEBPS/ch10.html#id287`.
