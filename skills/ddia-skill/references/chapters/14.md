# Chapter 14. Doing the Right Thing

Original applied guidance, not copied book text. These procedures are engineering deductions; retrieve the cited source sections before attributing a consequential claim to DDIA. Verify product/version guarantees independently.

## Source orientation

- Data systems have intended and unintended effects on people; their purpose and consequences belong in engineering decisions. Ethical reasoning is an iterative participatory practice rather than a compliance checklist.
- Predictive systems can reproduce bias present in training data and proxies. Population-level statistical accuracy does not make each individual decision correct. Responsibility, explanation, correction, and appeal must remain available.
- Feedback loops can amplify disadvantage or produce emergent outcomes. Consider the whole system, including people responding to the software, rather than optimizing an isolated metric.
- Privacy is control over what is disclosed, to whom, and for what purpose. Detailed tracking and derived profiles can exceed user expectations; nominal agreement is not necessarily meaningful choice.
- Data collection creates future misuse and breach exposure. Collect what the purpose needs and retain it only as long as justified. Deletion must include copies and derived representations, not merely the primary row.

## Purpose, affected people and proportional data use

Concept: `human-purpose`.

**Apply when:** Collect data or add prediction, personalization or surveillance.

**Procedure:** Identify the user benefit, affected people, power imbalance and less intrusive alternatives. Minimize fields and access, define accountable owners and revisit data purpose when use changes. Include people harmed by errors, not only the paying customer.

**Qualifications:** A technically available dataset is not automatically appropriate for a new use. Engineering analysis is not legal permission.

**Incorrect example:** Collect precise location forever because it might improve future models.

**Sufficient example:** Collect the least detail for a defined feature and set a justified retention/review period.

**Falsification check:** Remove unnecessary fields and test whether the feature still meets the actual requirement.

**Source sections:** `OEBPS/ch14.html#id393`, `OEBPS/ch14.html#id396`.

## Prediction errors, discrimination and feedback loops

Concept: `bias-feedback`.

**Apply when:** Use a score to allocate opportunities or trigger actions.

**Procedure:** Measure false positives/negatives and relevant subgroup outcomes, inspect proxy variables and selection bias, and analyze how decisions alter future training data. Separate model quality from acceptability of the decision policy.

**Qualifications:** A single aggregate accuracy metric can conceal serious uneven harm. Protected-category removal does not remove proxies or feedback effects.

**Incorrect example:** Train solely on previously approved applicants and interpret rejected groups as inherently unsuitable.

**Sufficient example:** Audit selection and feedback effects, evaluate meaningful error slices and provide human review where consequential.

**Falsification check:** Simulate policy feedback and evaluate errors on representative independently labeled populations.

**Source sections:** `OEBPS/ch14.html#id389`, `OEBPS/ch14.html#id390`, `OEBPS/ch14.html#id392`.

## Correction, explanation and appeal

Concept: `accountability`.

**Apply when:** Automate consequential decisions or provide consent controls.

**Procedure:** Name the decision owner, explain material inputs/limitations, retain suitable evidence and provide correction/appeal paths. Assess whether refusal is meaningful and whether corrections propagate to derived decisions.

**Qualifications:** A consent checkbox does not guarantee free choice. An appeal process that cannot change data or outcomes is cosmetic.

**Incorrect example:** Deny access with an unexplained score and route every appeal back through the same faulty record.

**Sufficient example:** Provide accountable review, correct authoritative facts and recompute dependent outcomes under a documented policy.

**Falsification check:** Inject a factual error and exercise the complete correction/appeal path, measuring downstream repair.

**Source sections:** `OEBPS/ch14.html#id391`, `OEBPS/ch14.html#id395`.

## Retention, deletion and derived copies

Concept: `privacy-lifecycle`.

**Apply when:** Set retention, implement deletion or persist immutable history.

**Procedure:** Inventory identifiers through source records, logs, replicas, indexes, exports, models, clients and backups. Specify erasure, tombstone, expiry and restore behavior by purpose. Minimize sensitive logging and validate permission boundaries.

**Qualifications:** Deletion from a primary table may leave accessible derived data. Immutable logs and backups need an explicit lifecycle; technical erasure is not a blanket compliance claim.

**Incorrect example:** Delete a profile row while restoring its searchable documents on the next rebuild.

**Sufficient example:** Carry the deletion policy through derivations and rebuild/restore procedures with documented retention exceptions.

**Falsification check:** Delete a test person, rebuild indexes and restore a backup in isolation; inspect all intended access paths.

**Source sections:** `OEBPS/ch14.html#id394`, `OEBPS/ch14.html#id460`.

## Jurisdiction, responsibility and governance limits

Concept: `law-society`.

**Apply when:** Make a legal/compliance claim or transfer personal data across regions.

**Procedure:** Identify jurisdictions, data purpose, affected parties and applicable organizational policy. Obtain current authoritative legal guidance for the actual decision. Separate technical controls, ethical judgment and legal interpretation in the evidence.

**Qualifications:** The book supplies analysis, not a current legal determination. Regulatory obligations depend on context and change over time.

**Incorrect example:** Declare a system compliant because encryption and a delete endpoint exist.

**Sufficient example:** Document verified requirements and map specific controls/evidence to them, using qualified review where needed.

**Falsification check:** Trace a concrete access/deletion/retention requirement through implementation and identify uncovered copies or unsupported claims.

**Source sections:** `OEBPS/ch14.html#sec_future_legislation`, `OEBPS/ch14.html#id397`.
