# Chapter 6. Replication

Original applied guidance, not copied book text. These procedures are engineering deductions; retrieve the cited source sections before attributing a consequential claim to DDIA. Verify product/version guarantees independently.

## Source orientation

- Replication supports read scale, locality, durability and availability but is not a backup: deletions and corruption can be copied too. Restore requires historical snapshots/logs and a tested recovery path.
- Synchronous acknowledgement waits for specified replicas and trades latency/availability for durability under the stated failures. Fully asynchronous acknowledgement can precede replication, so acknowledged writes can be lost at failover. The chosen successor and acknowledgement semantics matter more than the simple label replicated.
- Bootstrapping a follower needs a consistent snapshot tied to an exact log position, followed by catch-up. Retention balances recovery ability against disk exhaustion. Catch-up and failover add load when a system may already be struggling; fencing and correct membership prevent competing leaders.
- Read-your-writes, monotonic reads, and consistent-prefix reads prevent different user-visible anomalies. Asynchronous lag has no general time bound. Routing to a leader/up-to-date replica or tracking logical progress can improve guarantees, but cross-device sessions and failover complicate those techniques.
- Multi-leader replication can support local writes during inter-region or offline disconnection, but concurrent updates need conflict semantics. Last-write-wins converges by discarding updates and can misorder later writes when wall clocks disagree. A consistent merge is not automatically a correct business invariant; uniqueness, capacity, or nonnegative balances may require coordination.
- CRDT/OT techniques target convergent merges for suitable data types; manual sibling merging can resurrect deletions or create further conflicting resolutions. Causality is awareness/dependence, not wall-clock simultaneity. Version vectors capture concurrent versus superseding updates.
- Leaderless quorums trade latency, availability and stale-read risk. The condition w+r>n relies on overlapping replica sets and does not by itself provide linearizability under concurrent operations, partial writes, membership changes, clock errors or sloppy quorums. Read repair, hinted handoff and anti-entropy each have different catch-up behavior and operational cost.

## Leader acknowledgements, bootstrap and failover

Concept: `replication-durability`.

**Apply when:** Set replica acknowledgement policy, add followers or promote a leader.

**Procedure:** Trace acknowledged writes through replica positions and promotion eligibility. Establish a consistent snapshot/log boundary for follower creation. Include fencing, client rerouting and lost-acknowledged-write reconciliation. Compare physical/WAL, statement and logical logs for compatibility.

**Qualifications:** Async failover may lose acknowledged writes. Replication is not an independent backup against logical damage.

**Incorrect example:** Promote any reachable asynchronous follower and promise zero data loss.

**Sufficient example:** Promote according to an explicitly tested durability policy; disclose loss windows and reconcile business commitments.

**Falsification check:** Pause replication, acknowledge writes, fail over and compare visible committed identities and recovery behavior.

**Source sections:** `OEBPS/ch06.html#sec_replication_sync_async`, `OEBPS/ch06.html#sec_replication_new_replica`, `OEBPS/ch06.html#sec_replication_failover`.

## Read-your-writes, monotonic and prefix reads

Concept: `replication-reads`.

**Apply when:** Read from replicas after writes or display causally related records.

**Procedure:** State the exact user guarantee. Trace replica choice, log positions and cross-shard dependencies; compare leader reads, session affinity, position barriers or acceptable staleness. Separate observed lag from guaranteed lag bounds.

**Qualifications:** Low average lag is not a staleness guarantee. Monotonic reads do not alone preserve cross-record causality.

**Incorrect example:** Route a password change confirmation to an arbitrary lagging replica.

**Sufficient example:** Use an authoritative read or a verified replication-position barrier for that operation.

**Falsification check:** Delay selected replicas and alternate read destinations; check session and cross-record histories.

**Source sections:** `OEBPS/ch06.html#sec_replication_ryw`, `OEBPS/ch06.html#sec_replication_monotonic_reads`, `OEBPS/ch06.html#sec_replication_consistent_prefix`.

## Multi-leader and local-first synchronization

Concept: `multi-leader-sync`.

**Apply when:** Support offline edits or concurrent regional writers.

**Procedure:** Define offline requirements, authority per item, topology, identity and merge ownership. Trace reconnect, dependency ordering, deletes and schema upgrades. Decide which invariants require coordination versus explicit conflict resolution.

**Qualifications:** Offline availability cannot universally preserve arbitrary shared constraints. Local-first and ordinary caches have different ownership expectations.

**Incorrect example:** Allow offline clients to independently sell the same last unit, then call merged logs correct.

**Sufficient example:** Allocate exclusive rights or coordinate admission; allow mergeable offline edits only for semantics the product accepts.

**Falsification check:** Create concurrent edits/deletes on disconnected clients, reconnect in varied order and check both convergence and business constraints.

**Source sections:** `OEBPS/ch06.html#sec_replication_multi_leader`, `OEBPS/ch06.html#sec_replication_offline_clients`.

## Conflict semantics, causal versions and CRDTs

Concept: `conflict-resolution`.

**Apply when:** Resolve concurrent values or select last-write-wins.

**Procedure:** Define whether operations commute and whether concurrent intent must be retained. Compare application merge, version vectors, CRDT/OT or single-writer ownership. Include deletion semantics, causal metadata and repair.

**Qualifications:** Convergence is not preservation of the business invariant. Wall-clock last-write-wins can discard valid writes when clocks differ.

**Incorrect example:** Merge inventory counts using last-write-wins and assume no sale is lost.

**Sufficient example:** Represent a suitable commutative operation or coordinate scarce resources; retain conflicts where automatic resolution is semantically invalid.

**Falsification check:** Permute and duplicate concurrent operations with clock skew and deletions; check invariant as well as convergence.

**Source sections:** `OEBPS/ch06.html#sec_replication_write_conflicts`, `OEBPS/ch06.html#sec_replication_crdts`, `OEBPS/ch06.html#sec_replication_concurrent`.

## Leaderless quorums and repair

Concept: `leaderless-quorums`.

**Apply when:** Choose quorum sizes or infer consistency from overlap.

**Procedure:** Inspect failure, concurrency, sloppy quorum, partial-write and version-ordering assumptions. Trace read repair, hinted handoff and anti-entropy. Check actual product semantics before asserting linearizability.

**Qualifications:** The arithmetic w+r>n establishes overlap under its assumptions, not complete ordering or linearizability. Successful reads may need repair.

**Incorrect example:** Promise linearizable reads because read and write quorums overlap.

**Sufficient example:** State the narrower guarantee and use an actual linearizable protocol for uniqueness or ownership that requires it.

**Falsification check:** Construct concurrent and failed-write histories, node recovery and stale versions; check claimed read behavior.

**Source sections:** `OEBPS/ch06.html#sec_replication_quorum_condition`, `OEBPS/ch06.html#sec_replication_quorum_limitations`, `OEBPS/ch06.html#sec_replication_read_repair`.
