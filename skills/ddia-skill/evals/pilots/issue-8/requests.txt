Assess each supplied situation. Recommend a proportionate next step, identify any concrete failure trace, distinguish facts from unknowns, and state how to verify the recommendation. A. A team of two operates an internal research-labeling web app used by 35 people. PostgreSQL is already backed up and handles all app state. Peak writes are below 3 per second, p99 API response time is 120ms against a 500ms target, and daily CSV exports take 20 seconds. Exports are read-only; occasionally generating the same export twice costs a few cents. The proposed next sprint replaces the export cron script with Kafka, a durable workflow service, and Redis locks "so it is production ready." The team has no experience operating these systems. Decide what to build and what facts would justify a different design.

B. A two-region booking service has a last available seat. Both regions must accept reservations immediately during any inter-region network partition, and product requires that no confirmed seat is ever oversold. No per-region inventory rights are preallocated. A proposed design stores seats in independent regional databases and uses last-write-wins merging when the link recovers. An engineer says quorum writes and a distributed lock can satisfy all requirements without changing them. Review this proposal and give realistic choices.

C. A stream job bills device usage at the tariff effective when each measurement occurred. Measurements have unique tenant-scoped event IDs and event timestamps. Events can arrive 48 hours late, and operators replay the last 90 days after fixing calculation bugs. Tariff changes are keyed by tenant/device class and effective timestamp. A proposed job joins every measurement to the latest tariff in a compacted table, drops arrivals beyond a 1-hour watermark, deletes dedupe markers after 7 days, and appends charges to a sink. The sink can enforce uniqueness on tenant/event/calculation-version. Review the end-to-end design, including a safe recalculation and publication approach.

D. A service supports deleting a user's messages. It has authoritative rows, asynchronously maintained search and vector indexes, immutable event archives, derived training datasets, and backups. Its restore procedure currently restores a month-old database then rebuilds all indexes from the archive. Product says "delete the row and we are done, because derived data is not authoritative." No legal jurisdiction or retention policy has been specified. Propose an engineering deletion and recovery plan, indicate what remains a policy decision, and avoid claiming legal compliance.
